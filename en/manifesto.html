Manifesto

Most Bitcoin sites are trying to sell you something. A course. An exchange account. An ideology. The implicit question is always the same: should you buy? This site starts with a different question. Should you understand?

The best investment you make in your life is not buying a cheap asset and hoping it goes up. It's learning. Education has asymmetric risk in the best sense: huge upside, zero downside. Knowledge does not crash with the market.

In Bitcoin, blockchain, and Web3, this is more true than almost anywhere else.

The people who actually profited

Imagine 2013. Bitcoin trading at a hundred dollars. Two people are staring at the same screen.

The first buys. Bets on the price. If it goes up, they get rich. If it goes down, they lose.

The second spends two years studying. Reads the whitepaper until they understand it. Learns cryptography, hash functions, consensus protocols, the history of money. By 2015, in a casual conversation, they can answer what nobody around them can answer.

Jump to 2024. The first person has either made good money or lost it all, depending on when they bought and when they sold. The second person has started a company. Or become a consultant. A researcher. A reference. Or simply works as an engineer in a field that pays very well because they understand something few people genuinely understand.

And here is the point: even if Bitcoin had completely failed — even if the price were zero today — the second person would not be worse off. They would still understand cryptography, distributed systems, alternative monetary systems, internet protocols. Knowledge that compounds for the rest of a life.

Key insight
Buying Bitcoin is a bet. Studying Bitcoin is not. In every possible scenario, the one who studied comes out ahead.

Asymmetric risk

Studying Bitcoin is a low-risk, high-return investment in any scenario:

If Bitcoin succeeds
You are among the people who understand it from the inside. Career, company, consulting, research, anything.
If Bitcoin fails
You gained technical and economic knowledge that applies elsewhere. Cryptography, distributed systems, the history of money — they don't go anywhere.
If Bitcoin stays where it is
You are smarter about money, about how large systems coordinate without a central authority, and about how one of the most-discussed technologies of our time actually works.

In all three scenarios, you come out ahead. That is the kind of bet worth making.

That is why this site exists

I believe in Bitcoin. But what I actually advocate for is not buying Bitcoin. It is understanding Bitcoin.

This site is how I have found to make that understanding accessible. No hype, no faith, no mystery, and nothing to sell. You will leave here knowing enough to make your own decisions — about buying or not, about believing or not.

That is the real work. The rest is speculation.

— Renato Diniz
